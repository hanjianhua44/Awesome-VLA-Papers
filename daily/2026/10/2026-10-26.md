> Vision-language-action (VLA) models are increasingly used for robot manipulation because they map camera images and a language instruction directly to actions. Robots that cannot be tethered to a workstation, such as mobile manipulators and humanoids, must run them on onboard embedded boards, where the prefill latency of the language backbone grows with the hundreds of visual tokens processed at every control step. > Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models map a language instruction and the current observation directly to actions, without checking whether the instruction has been completed. For long-horizon tasks, a single failed step therefore goes unnoticed and leaves the whole task unfinished. Such failed episodes, however, often end with the task partially complete. ### 🔥 #9｜If at First You Don't Succeed, Backtrack: Lightweight Recovery for Frozen VLAs

**Thomas Landeg, Thierry Tambe** |  `Stanford, ETH Zurich, Univ of Zurich`

📎 [2610.32941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32941) | `cs.RO cs.LG`

🎯 **Robotics / VLA Architecture** · 综合分 `8.56`

> Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models perform well on robotic manipulation, yet remain brittle once their own actions carry them into states their training data barely covers. We introduce Backtrack, a lightweight failure detection and recovery mechanism for frozen VLAs that needs none of these. A small failure prediction head, trained only on outcome-labeled rollouts of the frozen policy, reads the VLA's existing internal representations to estimate whether the current episode will fail. >
