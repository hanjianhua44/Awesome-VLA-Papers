### 🔥 #1｜Neuro Inspired Robotics: What Robot Learning Can Borrow from the Sensorimotor Brain

**Ruihan Zhang, Davy Deng** |  `MIT`

📎 [2611.02510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02510) | `cs.RO cs.AI`

🎯 **Robotics / Data & Pre-training** · 综合分 `7.72`

> Robot policies still fail at everyday manipulation: an unfamiliar task, an object never held before, a scene that changes mid-motion, a skill that should improve with use. They fail despite pretraining on over a million trajectories, pooled from dozens of robots at great expense, while a child acquires all of these abilities within a few years of learning from a single body. This survey argues that the gap is one of design, and proposes designs for the next generation of robot models, drawn from how the nervous system solves the same problems. From the body we take compliance: the policy commands stiffness alongside position, so the controller yields where the task allows and presses where it must, which greatly lowers the demand for precise position output
>
> **关键技术：** 递归自我改进 (RSI) / 预训练

### 🔥 #4｜VLALagBench: Understanding When and Why VLA Inference Latency Matters

**Qi Wen, Borui Xu, Hussein Amro 等 (14 位作者)** |  `NVIDIA, Stanford, NTU, NUS`

📎 [2611.03103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03103) | `cs.RO`

🎯 **Robotics / VLA Architecture** · 综合分 `7.86`

> Vision-language-action (VLA) models offer a promising path toward general-purpose robot manipulation. While inference latency can affect task success in a constantly evolving physical world, the relationship between latency and task success rate remains poorly understood. Using VLALagBench, we find that even a modest inference latency of 100 ms can nearly halve task success on moving targets, with sensitivity varying substantially across tasks and policies.
