### 🔥 #1｜AeroRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

**Shiwei Wu, Yuwen Pan, Zhi Li 等 (7 位作者)** |  `Tsinghua, SCUT, SUSTech`

📎 [2611.24077](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24077) | `cs.CV cs.AI cs.RO`

🎯 **Robotics / Data & Pre-training** · 综合分 `10.47`

> Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs, i.e., actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches, which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. In this paper, we present AeroRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. Our key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision
>
> **关键技术：** 递归自我改进 (RSI) / VLA

### 🔥 #4｜RECLAIM: Robot-Vision-Informed Adversarial Patch Defense via Embodied Attention Feedback

**Jiayou Zhu, Peng Guo, Litian Zhang 等 (9 位作者)** |  `BUAA`

📎 [2611.24494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24494) | `cs.RO cs.CR cs.CV`

🎯 **Robotics / VLA Architecture** · 综合分 `9.58`

> Vision-language-action policies control embodied agents through a perception-action loop grounded in physical interaction, where adversarial patches can corrupt action predictions and thereby alter subsequent observations. To mitigate these threats, many existing defenses rely on policy adaptation or auxiliary models, but require additional training or inference. We introduce RECLAIM, a training-free, inference-time defense that uses a frozen VLA's own attention response to local visual interventions to guide purification during closed-loop embodied manipulation. Our key insight is that attention changes induced by local edits provide feedback for choosing where and how to purify as the robot interacts with its environment.
